# Proof, not spot check

<p className="lede">
Bounded model checking answers a weaker question than we want: no history <em>up
to length N</em> breaks the obligation. For this language's fragment that gap
closes, because a computable threshold makes "no counterexample found" mean
"none exists".
</p>

## Why a fixed bound is not enough

`examples/fundo.eleph` claims elite status at seven bookings when the fact
requires eight. A bound of six approves it:

```out
$ eleph check examples/fundo.eleph --bound 6

  ok? question(C, elite)  linha 11  -  resposta yes e verdadeira
  ok? question(C, elite)  linha 13  -  resposta no e verdadeira

SEM CONTRAEXEMPLO  -  dentro dos limites checados, que nao sao exaustivos aqui
```

Note the vocabulary. `ok?` rather than `ok`, and `SEM CONTRAEXEMPLO` rather
than `PROVADO`. The tool never claims more than it checked. A fixed bound of
six was the behaviour in 0.1.0, and it was unsound — this example is the reason
it changed.

## The last-occurrence lemma

The truth of a formula at the end of a history depends only on **the order of
the last occurrence of each atom**, plus, for atoms under a `count`, how many
times they occurred, capped at *n*+1.

Read the operators and it is almost immediate:

| Operator | What it actually asks of the history |
|---|---|
| `a(x)` | does a last occurrence of `a(x)` exist |
| `a since_not b` | does `a`'s last occurrence exist and come after `b`'s |
| `count a >= n` | how many occurrences, saturating at *n*+1 |

The boolean connectives compose, and a quantifier is a finite disjunction or
sum of instances that are themselves in the fragment.

## The threshold

Any configuration the lemma admits is realised by a history with one position
per atom, so

```text
N  =  sum over DISTINCT atoms a of k(a),    k(a) = 1, or n_max(a)+1 under a count
```

is a completeness threshold. Unsatisfiability up to `N` is unsatisfiability
outright.

Two details matter for it being usable rather than merely correct.

**The sum runs over distinct atoms, not syntax nodes.** `once(a) and count(a) >
2` asks for one occurrence and for three; three occurrences answer both.
Counting per node is still a valid threshold, just a needlessly large one — and
large thresholds are what make an exhaustive run unaffordable. Making this
change in 0.2.0 cut thresholds roughly threefold and made the suite faster.

**The domain is bounded too.** An object with no occurrence in the history
falsifies every atom naming it, so quantifying over the objects that appear is
the same as quantifying over an unbounded universe. `W` distinct objects
suffice: one witness per existential, *n*+1 per counting quantifier, plus the
obligation's own free variables.

Each obligation is checked at **its own** threshold, so one expensive fact does
not slow down the rest of the file:

```out
6 obrigacoes derivadas do texto do programa
cada uma checada no seu limiar de completude (o maior: 10 eventos) -- exaustivo

PROVADO  -  6 obrigacoes valem para TODO historico, de qualquer tamanho
```

## Outside the linear fragment

The hypothesis that can fail is that every `since_not` takes **atoms**. A fact
whose body puts a compound expression there leaves the fragment.

That case does not lose completeness, only its linear bound. Every past
operator has a one-step recurrence — `once` and `since_not` each carry one bit,
`count` a counter saturating at *n*+1 — so a formula with *k* temporal
subformulas is evaluated by a deterministic monitor over

```text
S  =  2^k  ×  product of (n_i + 2)
```

states. If some history reaches a violating state, one does so without
repeating a state, hence in fewer than `S` steps. So `S` is a completeness
threshold for the whole language.

Quantifiers multiply *k* by the domain size, since an instance per object is
tracked. That is exponential in principle and small in practice.

<Callout type="limit" title="When even that is unaffordable">
Past a ceiling of 4096, an exhaustive run is not worth the wait. The checker
then says the run was not exhaustive rather than pretending, and marks each
affected obligation with the reason:

```out
verificacao NAO exaustiva neste programa
  linha 11: limite pedido abaixo do limiar
```
</Callout>

## The vocabulary, exactly

| Verdict | What it means |
|---|---|
| `PROVADO` | every obligation holds for **every** history, of any length |
| `SEM CONTRAEXEMPLO` | nothing broke within the bounds checked, which were not exhaustive here |
| `REPROVADO` | at least one obligation has a counterexample, printed as a history |
| `ok` | this obligation was discharged at its completeness threshold |
| `ok?` | this obligation survived the bound it was given, which was below its threshold |

## Testing the claim itself

The threshold is a claim like any other, so there is an experiment that would
break it: **no verdict may change above the computed threshold.** It is tested
by re-checking every obligation of every example at bounds and domains above
it, in <Source file="tests/test_soundness.py" />.

The full argument, with proofs, is the module docstring of
<Source file="eleph/threshold.py" />. The two theorems are proved on paper and
tested rather than mechanised; a Lean 4 artifact for the ground fragment is the
next thing worth building, and is [where help is
wanted](/docs/contributing).
